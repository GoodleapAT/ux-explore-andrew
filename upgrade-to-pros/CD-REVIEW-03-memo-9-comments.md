## Comment 4 is a data change, and it is bigger than it looks

**Northgate has no CSR.** The worked example's cast has Priya Raman qualifying, calling, pricing,
presenting and capturing the decision, and the contractor is described as around forty staff sharing
**one sales desk**. **Andrew has chosen the S14 shape instead**, where Dana Whitlock takes the call
and Terrell Boyd sells.

**What it costs, and none of it is drawing:**

- **A new person in the cast**, and a name for them.
- **A hand-off beat between the intake call and Start selling** that the story does not have. That
  beat currently sits inside the twenty two day gap, which is the thing section D exists to show.
- **It changes what the gap is.** At the moment nothing happens for three weeks. With a CSR, the
  hand-off happens in there, and the gap becomes "waiting for a rep to pick it up" rather than
  "nobody did anything", **which is a different and more interesting problem.**
- **It makes the two scenarios more alike, not less.** S14 was chosen partly because it is a
  different organisation. If both run a CSR and an advisor, one axis of difference goes.

**Not written into the worked example yet.** It wants a ruling on the cast and on what the hand-off
beat is before the data changes, because the worked example has been rewritten four times today
already and a fifth should be worth it.

## Learned from

- **15 Sep 2026.** Andrew asked whether pressing Start selling on screen A leads to screen B. **It
  does not, and nothing on screen A leads anywhere.** The question exposed a missing affordance that
  the memo had flagged as uncomfortable in its argument column while still drawing it as the primary
  action. **Flagging a misleading control and then drawing it as the primary is having it both ways**,
  and a reader following the strip will read the flow the page implies rather than the caveat
  underneath it.
