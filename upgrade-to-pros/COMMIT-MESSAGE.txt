S15 rewritten and walked: memo 10, fourteen frames, and the money worked out

The scenario changed and most of this commit is the consequence.

S15 was rewritten on the team's own Confluence page, at Andrew's explicit
instruction, to version 15. Inbound phone call rather than Home App. Two trades
rather than three, both sold. A $500 deposit with $72,600 on completion rather
than three milestones. No change order and no HOA step. Permits on both jobs,
and both jobs running in the same week. Twenty one beats. The Notes and Entities
columns were cleared, so Joel's authored annotations are off the page and
recoverable only from history, and the five sections below the script each now
contradict it and carry a banner saying which parts are dead.

Memo 10 walks that script. Fourteen screens against twenty one beats, drawn only
where the page's UX Specific column asks for a screen. Six beats are deliberately
not drawn and are listed with reasons: the proposal, the tender and contract, the
hand-off, the lead closing, and the materials delivery.

Two format departures, both declared in the file. The beat text and its UX
instruction sit in a rail beside the mock rather than the argument sitting
underneath, because the point of the round is fidelity to the script rather than
an argument for a layout. And there is no [i] annotation anywhere, because the
request was mocks next to beats, so the argument lives under each mock in three
blocks: what was taken out, what needs a home, and the question.

The money was not specified and had to be read across the whole script. Only
beat 7 gives a figure, beat 8 names a deposit and a plan, and beat 11 says to use
judgement. It lands as five readings of one card: nothing owed at the customer,
not yet due, due but uninvoiced, invoiced, settled. The reading five screens now
rest on is that the payment plan lives at the project and carries both milestones,
and that an invoice is raised per milestone when its trigger fires. If that is
wrong, five sections are wrong.

Nine revisions on review, and five of them change component behaviour. Money
becomes a section in its own right on every screen that has it, headed simply
Money, and the card's own title comes off because the group heading says it
already. The proposal card is gone and its figures are a nested breakdown inside
the money card. The balance-due condition is nested rather than sitting in a
column of figures pretending to be one. Job statuses are badges. Create invoice
moves off the page header into the money card. The all/active/complete filter
came off. The transcript is back under Trades on the intake screen. Every Work
group is now Jobs. And what was left at the property came off the close screen,
because it outlives the project.

Findings worth more than the screens:

- "Due, no invoice" is a state the model does not have. The balance falls due on
  completion and the invoice is raised three days later, so for three days
  $72,600 is owed and nothing has asked for it. It is the only amber in the run.
- The project has no task list of its own, which the permit screen makes visible
  by putting two job checklists side by side with nothing belonging to the
  container. There are two permits in this script.
- The collapsible-section record contradicts itself across two screens in the
  same memo. One section open on a live call, both open on a roof.
- Money is absent from the job screen entirely, where memo 9 drew it read-only
  with a badge. Both cannot be right.
- No Waiting on card appears anywhere in fourteen screens, because nothing in
  this script is ever blocked.
- Two tender instruments on one plan. The deposit is a cheque and the balance a
  credit card, and the plan is badged Cash throughout.
- The job screen links to "what changed" on a scope refined after the hand-off,
  which nothing records. The link is a promise the model cannot keep.

Also in here:

- A new CD brief for memo 10, and the morning's brief marked superseded. Two
  briefs for the same scenario were live inside one day, built from two memos
  whose letters mean different screens. The memo-name prefix in the frame label,
  invented this morning, is what stops that being a silent collision, and it was
  needed within hours of being written.
- The worked example and the memo now disagree, and the file had no rule that
  could have prevented it. Its rule covers inventing a value; the story under it
  moved instead. Recorded, not yet fixed, and the brief tells CD to ignore the
  file, which is its whole purpose inverted. This is the largest outstanding item
  in the project.
- Permit dates in memo 10 corrected to a single day, seven places. The script
  merges applying and issuing into one beat; the mock had invented a week-long
  wait in a story whose premise is that nothing is blocked.
- A git lock rule. git status and git diff refresh the index as a convenience,
  which writes .git/index.lock, and the sandbox can create that file but cannot
  unlink it. Read git state with --no-optional-locks, always. The quirks table
  already had a row on diagnosing the lock, which was the wrong half of the
  problem.
- Two safeguards lost by writing our narrative onto their page. The drift
  register can no longer date-check S15, because our own edit moves the
  last-modified date, so for S15 only compare against version 15. And the
  deviations register's S15 rows are meaningless, because S15 is now our script
  and the gap the safeguard measured is gone.
- S15B marked superseded three hours after it was created, for the opposite
  reason it was created.
- Token file verified: 113 tokens, colour and elevation only, no radius, spacing
  or type scale. Two rounds overdue for re-extraction, so the workaround is now
  written into the brief.
