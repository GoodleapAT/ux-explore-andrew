# Drift check

**Everything we depend on that somebody else can change, and when we last looked.**

> **Who may write to this file:** any surface that can write. Update the **Checked** column every time
> you run the check. **Do not remove a row** because something is stable; stability is what the dates
> are for.

Our mocks, memos and registers are all built against sources that move: two Figma boards, seventeen
Confluence pages, a team repository and a design system. **Nothing tells us when one of them moves.**
This file is the substitute for that: a short routine somebody runs deliberately.

**It answers "has it changed", not "what changed".** That is the whole design. Asking whether a page
has moved is one cheap call; knowing what moved needs the old text stored, which is a different order
of effort. And once you know a scenario moved, you go and read it, so the cheap question does most of
the work.

---

## The routine

Ten minutes, and worth running at the start of any session that will build something.

1. **The team's model snapshot.** Check the last commit date on their board serialization in the team
   repository. If it is newer than ours, their model has moved and our diff is stale.
2. **Our own model.** Compare the read date below against the decision log. **If a decision postdates
   the read, the board is behind us**, which is the more common case and the one nobody expects.
3. **Each scenario we have used.** Fetch the page and compare its last-modified date with the date
   recorded below. Fetching the page metadata is enough; the body is not needed to answer the
   question.
4. **The design system and tokens.** Check whether the extracted stylesheet is still current, and
   whether the caveat about it being the mirror rather than Sol still holds.
5. **Report what moved**, update the dates, and add a changelog row naming which mocks are now
   suspect.

**A moved source does not mean a mock is wrong.** It means somebody has to look. Record what you
found in the deviations register rather than acting on it silently.

## What a drift finding looks like

Three kinds, and they are handled differently.

**The source moved and we agree.** Update the date, update whatever we derived from it, note it in the
changelog. The cheapest outcome.

**The source moved and it contradicts a decision.** Do not change the decision. Add a row to the
deviations register saying the scenario now says something our mock does not, and bring it to Andrew.
A scenario changing is not a decision being overturned.

**We moved and the source did not.** The common case, and the one this file exists to make visible.
Our board diagram is five decisions behind our own decision log. The fix is to redraw, and until then
anybody reading the board is reading history.

## The gap this register had, found 14 September 2026

**Every source listed above is an artefact.** A board, a snapshot, a Confluence page, a repository.
The change that did the most damage to our work arrived as **a decision taken in conversation**, which
no artefact reflected at the time and no ten-minute routine could have caught.

**So the register needs a row that is not an artefact:** the team's current position on anything we
hold a contrary position on. There is no URL for it and no date it was generated. The only way to
check it is to ask, which means the check is a conversation rather than a look.

**The practical consequence.** Where our model disagrees with theirs, the disagreement should be
re-confirmed before it is designed against, not after. We had three such rows and lost two of them
within a day of publishing a comparison that treated them as live.

## Where the team's position is now held

**One file, `TEAM-POSITIONS.md`, rows T-1 upward.** That is the answer to the gap recorded above. The
non-artefact source now has a home, a date per row and a history table, so "when did we last look"
is answerable for a conversation as well as for a board.

**Also worth recording: their own repository is behind their board.** The generated snapshot in the
team repository is still DRAFT v3 of 8 September. **v4 exists on the board and nowhere else**, so
there is no text source for it and no git diff to read. Everything we have from v4 was read off a
rendered screenshot.

**Two things in v4 we could not read** and should pick up if the wording matters: the mint edge label
is clipped in the render and reads only as far as "intent to sell to customer is ex", and an
Ownership versus Provenance panel is referenced in the visual language note but is not in the node we
looked at.
