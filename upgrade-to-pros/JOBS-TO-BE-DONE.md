# Jobs to be done

**What people are trying to accomplish, independent of any screen that helps them do it.**

> **Who may write to this file:** any surface that can write. Add a row to the index and a section
> below. A job to be done changes rarely; the evidence under it grows.
>
> **In the project rather than global, for now.** These are about contractors selling and delivering
> home improvement work, so they would hold for any project about the same people. There is only one
> such project today, and a global file that one project uses is clutter. Promote it when there are
> two.

**A job to be done is not a feature and not a persona.** It is a situation somebody is in, something
they are trying to achieve, and what they want to be true afterwards. **One job usually spans several
roles**, which is the point: the office manager and the crew lead can be doing the same job from
different ends.

Written as: **when** situation, **I want to** motivation, **so I can** outcome.

## The fields

| Field | What it means |
|---|---|
| **The job** | The when, want, so that sentence |
| **Roles** | Who does it. Several is normal |
| **Evidence or rationale** | One of the two, labelled, never both. Evidence quotes a person, a scenario beat or something observed. Rationale states the reasoning. The same discipline the leads use |
| **Scenarios** | Which of the seventeen exercise it |
| **Solutions** | Which of our designs address it. **Filled in later**, and the cross-reference is the point of this file |
| **Not this** | The nearest thing it is often confused with |

## J-01 · Decide which of several wants to pursue, and how

**When** a customer has raised more than one thing they might want, **I want to** decide which to
pursue and whether to pursue them together, **so I can** put one conversation in front of them
instead of three.

- **Roles.** Sales consultant. Owner, in a small shop where they are the same person.
- **Evidence.** The multi-trade scenario: three trades arrive over three weeks by three routes, and
  on 12 August the contractor decides to pursue all three together rather than one at a time. The
  scenario records that decision as leaving no trace, which is what makes it worth designing for.
- **Scenarios.** S15, S16.
- **Not this.** Not qualifying a lead. The decision here is about bundling, not about whether any one
  of them is real.
- **Solutions.** The pursuit moment, treatments B and C.

## J-02 · Keep work the customer wanted but did not buy

**When** a customer says yes to some of what I offered and not all of it, **I want to** keep the rest
alive with its price and the reason it was declined, **so I can** come back to it when the reason has
changed rather than starting cold.

- **Roles.** Sales consultant, owner.
- **Evidence.** The customer takes siding and windows and defers a $38,900 roof to spring, saying it
  is not leaking yet. Every model on the table except ours loses that roof once the session ends.
- **Scenarios.** S15, S16.
- **Not this.** Not a lost deal. Deferred and lost need to be distinguishable, and the team's own
  notes say so.
- **Solutions.** Lead status of deferred with a required reason, a note and a revive date. **Not yet
  solved:** nothing makes the revive date arrive.

## J-03 · Sell several trades as one decision without pricing them as one

**When** I am putting an offer together across more than one trade, **I want to** price each trade on
its own and choose which to show and in what order, **so I can** move within the conversation without
reopening the whole thing.

- **Roles.** Sales consultant.
- **Evidence.** All three trades priced three days before the visit. Roofing and siding offered
  first, windows priced and deliberately held back, then raised second. The held-back trade wins and
  the offered one loses, which only works if they were priced separately.
- **Scenarios.** S15, S16, S7.
- **Not this.** Not good, better and best, which is several ways to do one trade.
- **Solutions.** Options with a presentation state. **Not yet drawn:** the proposal view.

## J-04 · Know what is owed and what has been collected, at a glance

**When** somebody asks me where a job stands financially, **I want to** see the total, what has been
collected and what remains in one place, **so I can** answer without opening three records.

- **Roles.** Office manager, owner.
- **Evidence.** Every scenario in the set exercises this, and the payments-only tier is a product
  where this is the whole product.
- **Scenarios.** All seventeen.
- **Not this.** Not profitability. What is owed and what it cost are different questions with
  different answers.
- **Solutions.** The money card at the project.

## J-05 · Find out what is actually stopping a job

**When** a job has not moved for a while, **I want to** see what is holding it and who can move it,
**so I can** tell the difference between somebody being late and nobody being able to act.

- **Roles.** Production coordinator, crew lead, owner.
- **Evidence.** A windows job waits twenty six days in plan review with nothing in its own record
  saying so, because the blocking fact lives on the permit. The team's scenarios flag readiness
  gating repeatedly as their most expensive gap.
- **Scenarios.** S16 most sharply, and the team names it across the set.
- **Not this.** Not a task list. A task is work somebody controls; this is about the things nobody
  here can accelerate.
- **Solutions.** The waiting-on card, and a derived blocked state pointing at its blocker.

## J-06 · Hand a signed sale to the people who will deliver it

**When** a customer signs, **I want to** turn what was sold into work somebody can start, **so I can**
stop holding the detail in my head.

- **Roles.** Sales consultant handing over, production coordinator receiving.
- **Evidence.** The hand-off beat: one signature producing two jobs on two tracks with two crews, one
  of them a subcontractor.
- **Scenarios.** S15, S16, S1, S2.
- **Not this.** Not scheduling. The hand-off is the moment of translation, not the moment of booking.
- **Solutions.** One job per committed scope component, created at hand-off.

## J-07 · Walk into a conversation knowing what was said last time

**When** I am about to call or visit a customer, **I want to** see what has been said, promised and
noticed across everything we have done for them, **so I can** avoid asking a question somebody
already answered.

- **Roles.** Sales consultant, technician, office manager.
- **Rationale.** Nobody has asked for this in those words. It is inferred from the repeated pattern
  of a prior job, a note, and a suggestion all mattering to the next conversation, and from the
  request for a fuller history covering notes, objects and communications.
- **Scenarios.** S15 and S16 both open with a returning household. S8 and S13 turn on a technician
  knowing what was noticed last visit.
- **Not this.** Not an audit log. The question is what a person needs to know, not what the system
  did.
- **Solutions.** The activity and history cards. **Blocked:** communications have no entity.

## J-08 · Get paid the moment the work earns it

**When** a piece of work reaches a point the customer agreed to pay at, **I want to** bill for it
immediately and have the money reconcile itself, **so I can** stop funding the job out of my own
pocket.

- **Roles.** Owner, office manager.
- **Evidence.** Milestone structures appear in most of the set, and the financed telling makes it
  sharper still, with a lender disbursing against work evidence per job.
- **Scenarios.** S1, S2, S3, S15, S16, S14B.
- **Not this.** Not invoicing on a schedule. The trigger is a work event, not a date.
- **Solutions.** Parts on the master invoice, each carrying its own trigger.

## J-09 · Keep a relationship earning after the job closes

**When** a job finishes, **I want to** leave something in place that brings the customer back, **so I
can** stop buying every next job as a new lead.

- **Roles.** Owner, technician.
- **Evidence.** A maintenance plan sold at the closeout of the HVAC job, then twelve invoices
  spanning three later journeys and outliving all of them. The technician who spots a failing part on
  a routine visit is the same mechanism.
- **Scenarios.** S8, S9, S13, S17.
- **Not this.** Not a warranty, which is an obligation rather than a relationship.
- **Solutions.** Service as its own project type. **Provisional:** almost nothing about services is
  settled.

## J-10 · Start using the product without adopting all of it

**When** I only want the part of this that solves my immediate problem, **I want to** use that part on
its own and have it work properly, **so I can** decide later whether the rest is worth it.

- **Roles.** Owner, at a small contractor.
- **Evidence.** The two smallest scenarios: a card-only fence and deck crew with no CRM at all, and a
  solo handyman whose invoice is created after both the work and the payment. Both are drawn as real
  products rather than degraded ones.
- **Scenarios.** S5, S11, S6, S4.
- **Not this.** Not a free tier. These are real customers running real businesses on one capability.
- **Solutions.** The three product tiers, and the rule that a component declares what it does when its
  subject does not exist at a tier.

## J-11 · Update several things a customer is talking about, in one conversation

- **The job.** When I am on the phone with a customer who mentions three different pieces of work, I
  want to capture and update all of them where I am, so that I am not switching between views while
  somebody is talking to me.
- **Roles.** CSR, sales consultant.
- **Evidence.** **The team's stated reason for rejecting one lead per trade**, 14 September 2026, recorded as T-1: a CSR
  discussing several trades with a customer would otherwise have to keep several tabs or views open to
  update each one. This is the first job in this file that arrived as an argument against one of our
  positions rather than out of a scenario, and it won.
- **Scenarios.** S15 and S16, the multi-trade cases, on the intake call rather than at the sale.
- **Solutions.** The engagement, which is the team's answer. **Not yet drawn**, and drawing it is what
  the next round of UX work is for.
- **Not this.** Not J-01, which is about deciding which wants to pursue. This one is about the
  mechanics of a single conversation, and it is satisfied or broken by how much is reachable from one
  place.

**Why it is worth recording as a job rather than only as a decision.** The decision says a lead is an
engagement. The job says what the engagement has to be good at, which is the thing a screen can be
judged against. If a design satisfies the decision and fails this job, the decision was implemented
and the point was missed.

## What this file is for, eventually

**Cross-referencing solutions against jobs.** Every design we settle on should be answerable to at
least one of these, and any job with no solution against it is either not really a job or is a gap in
the work. Neither is visible until both lists exist, which is why the Solutions field is there
half-empty.
