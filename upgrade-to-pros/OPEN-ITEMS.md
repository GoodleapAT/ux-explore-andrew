# What is open

**One index of everything unresolved, so a cold session does not have to reconstruct it from nine
files. 15 September 2026.**

> **Who may write to this file:** any surface that can write. **This is an index, not a store.** Each
> row points at where the thing actually lives. Do not restate the detail here, because a restatement
> is a copy and copies go stale.
>
> **Close a row by deleting it and recording the closure where the item lives.** A closed item with a
> tick still costs a reader a decision about whether to read it.
>
> **Enforced properly on 15 September 2026.** Seven rows had been marked Closed and left in place,
> each with a note saying it was listed so nobody would reopen it. **Six of them were sitting in a
> section headed "Blocking the next round of design", answering the column "Why it blocks" with the
> word "Closed".** All seven are deleted and their closures are in the project decision log, dated.
> **The urge to leave a tick behind is the thing this rule exists to resist.**

## Why this exists

By the end of 14 September, open questions were spread across the positions file, two memos' to-decide
cards, the object register, the vocabulary, the ideas catalogue, the in-depth documents, the review
responses and the deviations register. **Nothing answered "what is still open", which is the first
question a new session asks.**
