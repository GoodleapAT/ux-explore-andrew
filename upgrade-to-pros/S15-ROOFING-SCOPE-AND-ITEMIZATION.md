# S15 roofing: scope of work and itemization

**Content for the two Pros Web fields, at both versions. 17 September 2026.**

> **All of this is OURS.** The data pack is explicit: *"No roofing line items exist anywhere"* and
> *"if a screen needs a breakdown it has to be invented and flagged."* The only figure below that
> comes from the script is the total, **$38,900**. Everything under it was constructed to reconcile
> to that total and nothing else validates it.

---

## Read this first: the price does not survive contact with a real roofer

$38,900 over 2,410 square feet is **$16.14 per square foot, or about $1,620 per roofing square.**
A tear-off and architectural shingle replacement in Sacramento runs roughly $550 to $900 per square
retail. **The scenario's roofing price is somewhere around double a market rate.**

Neither lever fixes it cleanly.

- **The price is SCRIPT** and locked. It is half of the $73,100 contract that runs through every
  money state in the scenario.
- **The area is OURS** and could be raised, but it cannot be raised enough. To reach a believable
  rate the roof would need to be about 48 squares, which on a 2,140 square foot two storey house
  with a footprint near 1,070 square feet is not a roof anybody would believe either.

So the itemization below is built at the script's total and is priced as a premium package, which
narrows the gap without closing it. **Any contractor shown this demo will notice.** The decision to
make is whether that matters for a demo about workflow, or whether the scenario's price wants
revising before the numbers go on a screen.

---

## The constraint the two versions reveal

**Refinement redistributes detail underneath a fixed total.** Sara signed $38,900 on 6 August, so
when Marcus refines the scope on 7 August he can add rows, split them and re-categorise them, but he
cannot change the sum. Changing the sum is a change order, and S15 has none.

That is worth building into the component: **the itemization total is locked by the contract, and
the only thing that unlocks it is a change order.** Every line below maps to a line in the sold
version, and the two totals are identical.

### The two lines that moved, and why they cancel

The sold version allowed **12 sheets of decking** and **3 days of site protection**. On the post-sale
inspection Marcus saw staining in the attic over the south slope, where the storm lifted the
shingles, and raised the allowance to **16 sheets**. He also cut site protection to **2 days**,
because the install is a two day job and the third day was a sales estimate rather than a plan.

| | Signed | Refined | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Decking allowance | $1,500 | $1,760 | +$260 |
| Two storey access and site protection | $6,300 | $6,040 | &minus;$260 |
| **Everything else** | | | unchanged |
| **Total** | **$38,900** | **$38,900** | **nil** |

**This is the normal case, not the exception.** Sold lines are round numbers built from allowances
and refined lines are real quantities, so they rarely match one for one. What is held is the total.
The surface should show per-line variance without alarm, and warn only when the sum moves.

## What this raises

1. **Does the job own an itemization, or read the proposal's?** The refined version is the crew's
   document and the sold version is the customer's. If they are one entity with two views, the job
   page shows a filtered read. If they are two records, somebody has to keep them reconciling, and
   the mapping table above is the evidence of how much work that is.
2. **Where does the change order attach?** The decking allowance is the only place in this job where
   the total could move, and S15 never exercises it. The model attaches a change order to one scope,
   which suggests the job, but the contract total lives at the project.
3. **The $1,060 permit line is a cost passed through at a markup nobody has specified.** A real
   itemization usually separates the municipal fee from the coordination labour, and this does not.
4. **Nothing here carries cost.** These are sell prices. Per-job cost is unavailable by rule, so the
   itemization cannot be the place margin gets calculated, which is what most contractors would
   expect it to be.
5. **The workmanship warranty has no term.** The scenario registers a material warranty and says
   nothing about labour, so the scope-of-work text above names only the manufacturer's. If Northgate
   offers a workmanship term, it belongs in the scope and would need inventing.
