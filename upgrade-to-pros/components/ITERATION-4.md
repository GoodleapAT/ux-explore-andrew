## The reason this file matters more than the records in it

**This is the first time a scenario has been composed rather than drawn**, which is the condition
global principle G-1 set before anything could be justified on its grounds. Twelve screens, and the
question was never whether they looked right. It was whether one library carried a single trade sale
with the money off and a three trade sale with the money on **without breeding near identical
siblings.**

**The answer is mostly yes, with one clear failure and one thing that died.** Both are below.

## What carried both scenarios unchanged

**Eight components, no amendments, no variants.** This is the evidence, such as it is. **It was nine
until Andrew's review**, which removed the only use of SubordinateList from the memo.

| Component | What it carried in S15 that it had not carried before |
|---|---|
| **PeerRow** | **Opportunities, leads, jobs, proposal options, stages and dependencies. Six kinds of thing across twelve screens.** The only thing that changed between them is the status line, which is what the record said it was for |
| **FactGrid** | The property, the call detail, the deferral, the project attributes |
| **The card shell and pairing** | Every screen. Two abreast held throughout, including where one card of a pair was empty |
| **Segmented control** | Opportunity filters on the customer record, and job filters on the project. **Counts in the labels every time.** It was also carrying the trade questions during a call until Andrew's review replaced that with collapsible sections |
| **Timeline** | How the project came together, and the opportunity's own history. **Two different objects, one component** |
| **SheetShell** | Start selling |
| **Field and CaptureFieldSet** | The call capture, including the not-asked treatment |
| **Empty state** | Four times, and it grew a footer. See the one amendment below |

**PeerRow is the finding.** A demand record, a selling record and a work record are different objects
at different grains, and **all three are one row with a different status line.** If the library
proliferates anywhere it should have proliferated here, and it did not.

**SubordinateList is now unexercised, and that is worth watching.** It was named on 14 September after
appearing twice in one memo, and its only use in memo 9 was the two other trades nested inside the
call card. **Andrew's review replaced that region with collapsible sections**, so the component is
currently used nowhere in the current S15 material. It is still used on the S14 canvas, for potential
matches inside the capture form, so it is not dormant. **But a component that loses its second use
within a day of being named is a component to check on**, because it may have been named too early.

## What died

### Lineage, and it was built for this scenario

**The diagram of how a project was assembled: leads on the left, container in the middle, jobs on the
right.** Its own record says it does not render below two leads or two jobs.

**S15B has one lead.** So the component built for the multi-trade case cannot render in the
multi-trade case. Section H replaced it with a six row timeline, which says the same thing in less
space and needs no orthogonal connectors.

**This is what a model change does to a library**, and it is worth seeing plainly: the component was
correct, it stayed correct, and the shape it drew stopped existing. **Not a defect and not a
deprecation. Recommend marking it dormant** rather than removing it, because the financed telling and
the cross-time case may bring two leads back.

## What failed

### SubStatusRow, in the opposite direction to the one its record predicted

**Its record warns:** "In this scenario there is no permit, no financing and no home owners
association at this address, so the card has one row at step 8. A card that exists to prove a
distinction, with one row in it, may not earn its place. Below two rows, consider folding them into
the stage card."

**S15B gives it four rows and all four are settled.** HOA approved, materials due, insurance on file,
permit not required. **So the card reads as four pieces of good news** on a page where nothing is
waiting for anything.

**The prediction was that too few rows would kill it. The actual failure is that a full card with
nothing blocking says nothing.** The distinction it exists to draw, between what the team controls
and what it cannot, is invisible when nothing is stuck.

**Recommend:** the card collapses to a line when its blocking count is zero, in the same way
ReadinessCard puts the count in its segment label. **Not recorded as an amendment yet**, because it
is a proposal and Andrew has not seen it.

## Amendments

### The opportunities card splits in two

**Amended, from Andrew's review of memo 9.** The card that listed all opportunities with a segmented
control becomes **two cards**: a full-width lone card headed **On this lead**, and below it a pair
whose first card is headed **Other opportunities at this property**. **Not "worth raising"**: on that screen nothing has been marked yet, and the footer is what offers the marking.

**The difference between demand being sold and a trade somebody thinks might sell is now
structural**, not a phrase in a status column. It reads better and it exercises the lone-card rule of
14 September for the first time deliberately.

**What it costs, and the cost is delayed.** The filter has gone. With two or three opportunities
nothing is lost. **With a household carrying a dozen, including dismissed ones, neither card can hide
anything**, and dismissed opportunities now have no home on that screen at all. **Open: at what count
does the second card need a filter back.**

### Empty state gains a footer

**Adapted.** An empty card now carries a count of what came before and a link to it: "1 closed
project, 1 job / See previous work", left aligned with the link beside it.

**From Andrew's review comment of 15 September.** It appears four times in memo 9 and it is the
difference between a card that says nothing exists and a card that says nothing exists **yet**, which
on a returning household are very different claims.

**Absorbs** any count, any number of links, and an empty state with no history at all, where the
footer is absent rather than reading zero.

### Template: customer record

**Amended.** Gains a **Selling** group pairing Opportunities with Leads, and Service plans moves into
Operations. Jobs nest under projects. Latest activity is two deep with a fade. Active projects shows
a placeholder with a previous-work footer.

**The record's old claim that "nothing indents under anything" is dead**, and was already dead before
this memo: the no-indenting rule fell on 14 September.

**The new state to declare:** a customer with opportunities and no demand. **A returning household
carries recommendations with no lead, no project and no job**, and that is now the most common state
this template will be in.

### Template: project workspace

**Amended, and this is the one to watch.** Two changes, both from today's rulings.

**Its thinnest state now has to last weeks.** It was designed for a container that is empty for
minutes, which is what S14 does. In the first version of S15B it was empty for twenty two days and somebody opened it
repeatedly. **A state nobody dwells in and a state somebody lives in are not the same design**, and
the record did not distinguish them.

**Andrew shortened the story rather than solve it, 15 September.** The gap is six days now, so the
problem is much smaller. **It has not gone away**: it will return the first time a real lead sits,
and the template still has no declared state for a container that somebody looks at repeatedly while
it holds nothing.

**And it loses its lead partway through, which is the one that is left.** From the hand-off the project has no lead behind it, so
the only route back to how the sale happened is one row in an attributes card. **Declare a state for
after the lead closes**, because five of the eleven screens are in it.

## New

## CollapsibleSection

**Level** molecule, a section header with a body that opens and closes. **New** in memo 9, section B,
**and it is a declared departure from a Default rule.**

One trade's questions inside a capture card. A header carrying the trade and how much of it has been
answered, and a body that opens.

**States** **open**, drawn on roofing; **collapsed**, drawn on siding and windows, where the header
carries the count and nothing else; and **empty**, where a trade has no questions configured and the
section does not render.

**Use it** where somebody is capturing structured information **while somebody else talks**, and the
person cannot control the order the information arrives in. **That condition is the whole
justification.** A sheet would cover the transcript and a segmented control hides two trades behind a
click.

**Do not use it** anywhere the person controls the order, which is almost everywhere. **The
composition standard still says detail goes sideways into a sheet, not down into an accordion**, and
this is an exception with two conditions rather than a weakening of that rule.

**Two conditions, and they are part of the component.** The **counts move onto the collapsed
headers**, so the card answers its question before anything is opened. And **one section is open by
default**, so the card never presents as three closed bars.

**When its subject is absent**, at a contractor with one trade, there is one section and it is always
open, **at which point it should not be a collapsible section at all** and the questions go straight
into the card. That is the S14 case.

**Absorbs** any number of sections, a count or a temperature on the header, and a section that cannot
be opened because nothing is configured for it. **A new component instead** if two sections ever need
to be open at once, because that is a different interaction and a different height problem.

**Tiers** unchanged wherever demand is captured.

**What it cost, recorded because it is a real loss.** The nested block that used to show what the
customer said about the other two trades has gone. **The page no longer says on its face that Sara
was keen on the siding and lukewarm on the windows**; that is now behind two closed headers.

## CallTranscript

**Level** organism, meaning a card with its own content stream rather than a row or a field.
**New** in memo 9, section B.

What is being said while a call is happening, shown beside the form somebody is typing into.
Timestamped lines, the other party's lines set in secondary text, the newest at the foot.

**States** **live**, drawn in section B, where lines are arriving; **ended**, where the call is over
and the transcript is a record; **unavailable**, where the call is not being transcribed; and
**absent**, where the organisation has no transcription at all.

**Use it** where somebody is capturing structured information while somebody else talks, and losing
what was said is the expensive failure. **Only ever beside a capture surface**, never on its own.

**Do not use it** as a record of a call after the fact. That is a note, or it is communications,
which is still a register row. **And do not let anything on the page depend on it**, because the
capability does not exist and the whole component may go.

**When its subject is absent** the card is **absent** and the capture surface takes the full width.
It must not degrade to an empty panel, because a transcript region with no transcript on a screen
somebody is typing into is the worst version of this.

**Absorbs** any number of lines, speaker attribution, a scroll, and the ended and unavailable states.
**A new component instead** the moment anything in the transcript becomes actionable, for instance a
phrase that can be promoted into a field, because that is a capture surface and this only reports.

**Tiers** unchanged across all three, wherever calls are taken.

**This record carries a warning rather than a rule break.** The capability is in the object register
with hold: watch, and **hold: watch exists precisely because if transcription is real the capture
screen is a different design.** Do not build on this record until somebody establishes whether calls
are recorded at all.

## What the memo did not test, and it is half the point

**Money.** Placeholdered on six of eleven screens at Andrew's instruction. **MoneyCard and
OffSystemMoneyCard are still two components for one job**, differing only in whether the product does
the collecting, and that is the clearest near-identical sibling in the library. **S14 exercises one
and S15 exercises the other, which is exactly the comparison that would settle it**, and it has now
been deferred twice.

**Recommend:** the next round is money, on both scenarios, and nothing else.

## Learned from

- **15 Sep 2026.** **Composing a scenario rather than drawing it is what G-1 asked for and this is the
  first time it happened.** The library carried nine components across both scenarios unchanged, which
  is weak evidence for a strong claim, but it is the first evidence of any kind.
- **15 Sep 2026.** **PeerRow carried six kinds of object on eleven screens with only the status line
  differing.** If the anti-proliferation claim is ever going to be demonstrated, that is what it looks
  like.
- **15 Sep 2026.** **A component can be correct, stay correct, and have the shape it draws stop
  existing.** Lineage was built for a multi-lead project and the model no longer has one. That is a
  third category alongside amendment and deprecation, and the library standard does not have a word
  for it.
- **15 Sep 2026.** **The seventh field predicted the wrong failure for SubStatusRow.** It warned about
  too few rows and the real failure is a full card with nothing blocking. **The field works; the
  prediction inside it was a guess**, and it is worth saying that absorption is a claim to test rather
  than a fact to rely on.

## Added after Andrew's second review, 15 September 2026

**Two screens went on the front and the money went in.** The memo is twelve screens now, not ten, and
five of them carry money. **Three more components were exercised and none of them needed changing.**

| Component | What it carried |
|---|---|
| **MoneyCard** | **Four states across five screens:** no tender chosen at the proposal, cash part paid at the hand-off and again during the work, and settled at the close. **The same card, four readings, no variants** |
| **Job money, read-only** | On the siding job during the change order, with a badge saying where it is held. **The component record required both halves of that** and this is the first time either has been drawn |
| **Duration badge** | "11 months" on two opportunities that have been open since September 2025. **Absorbed by the existing badge** with no new component |

**And the money answer, which was the point.** MoneyCard held four states with nothing more than
different row labels and one badge. **OffSystemMoneyCard exists as a separate component for a story
where the product does not collect**, and on this evidence that difference is a state and not a
component. **Recommend merging them**, with the tender as the state axis: none chosen, cash, financed,
off-system, settled. **It is a recommendation and not an amendment**, because CD is better placed to
answer it with both scenarios in real components, and the brief asks it to.

**The two new screens exercised nothing new**, which is the most useful thing about them. A customer
record at rest and the same record ninety seconds after an inbound form, both built from cards,
pairs, PeerRow, the empty state and the badge. **The library drew a state nobody had thought about
without being told anything.**
