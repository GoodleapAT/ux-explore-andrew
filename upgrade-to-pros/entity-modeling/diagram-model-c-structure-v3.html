The project is the container

One customer, holding properties, opportunities and a customer-level record, and a mandatory project. Inside the project, selling and work are two groupings in sequence: selling carries the proposals and the contract; work carries the jobs. The money sits at project level, under both.

Selling and work are not proposed as entities. They are groupings in the experience. The claim is that in several parts of the product a person needs a clear line between what is being sold and what is being delivered, not that the data model gains two objects.

Selling and work are groupings, not objects. Everything else drawn here is proposed as an entity; those two are drawn because the experience needs the distinction, and they are the one place this diagram describes a screen rather than a structure.

The pipeline is not drawn, deliberately. Stages, statuses, tasks, gates and checklists are cross-cutting rather than owned by any one card. They are seen and acted on at the project, selling, work and job levels alike, so boxing them at one level would understate where they appear.

A good deal is deliberately left out. Documents, notes, measurements, history, site assessments and insights attach to more than one object — not only the customer, but the project, selling, work, jobs and money too. Drawing them at a single level would be a claim this model is not making. Complete on structure, not exhaustive on contents.

Selling and work are two cards inside the project rather than a flat sequence, which makes the split visible: selling runs whether or not anything is bought, work only exists once something is. The single arrow between the cards is the only place one object produces another. The money has its own colour because it belongs to neither card: it is contained by the project and sits under both.
