Pros WebWhy the model has to changeMemo, 31 Aug 2026

The model describes a transaction. The business is a relationship.

Merlin's contractor-facing data model is a coherent shape for one sale, to one address, on one document. Every deficiency below is a version of that shape being asked to hold a second sale. It follows one customer of Northgate Home Services, drawn twice on the same day. This memo makes the case that the fix is structural rather than visual, and it is written for two audiences at once: the first half is the symptom, which anyone can see, and the second half is the diagnosis, which needs the code.

[i] Inside a mockup, small grey text prefixed with [i] is my annotation, not interface copy. Everything else in a mockup frame is intended as real UI.
How to read thisTwo halves, two readers ▸

Sections A and B are the symptom. One customer, one afternoon, drawn twice: once on the record we have and once on the record we want. If you are here for whether the work is worth doing, these two screens are the argument and you can stop after them.

Sections C and D are the diagnosis. What is actually in the codebase, and what the words we use are doing to us. If you are going to build this, section C is the part that decides which options are available at all.

Taken as settled for this memoWorking assumptions, not decisions anyone has signed ▸
  • Contractors want to hand a customer one document for work sold in one conversation, and need to run it as separate work afterwards. Different crews, different timelines, different permits.
  • A customer is a person, above the address, and can hold several properties.
  • The single-sale case must stay exactly as simple as it is today. Any fix that taxes the contractor who only ever sells one thing at a time is not a fix.
  • The first four rows in section C are read out of the Merlin codebase, not inferred from documentation. The fifth is design context and is labelled as such. Several relevant Confluence pages are four to ten months old and contradict what ships.
The one thing to decide before anything is builtNot answered by this memo ▸

Does a proposal hold several priced scopes, or do proposals stay whole and something new gathers them? Scopes inside one proposal give the customer one document, one signature and one financing decision, and extend an object that already exists. Keeping proposals atomic means inventing the thing that gathers them, and the customer signs several times.

Both branches are real work and they are not the same work, which makes this a development-shape question as much as a design one. Every screen in this memo looks the same either way, so nothing here settles it. If it goes unanswered, the next round builds on a guess.

THE FAILURE IS REPETITION WITHOUT A CONTAINER

Four proposals, two loans and a cash sale, three contracts, three invoices. Every one of those objects is correct on its own. The problem is that there is no level between the customer and the proposal, so the page has no way to group them and no vocabulary to say which invoice belongs to which piece of work.

This is not a layout problem that better design solves. Any arrangement of these objects has the same defect, because the relationship the contractor is holding in their head does not exist in the data.

THE CUSTOMER FELT IT BEFORE THE CONTRACTOR DID

Two loan applications and two signing ceremonies for one afternoon is a worse sales experience than the contractor's paper process. It is also the thing the contractor most wanted the software to fix, and the reason they mention bundling unprompted.

THE PAGE IS ALREADY AT ITS LIMIT

This is a modest customer. Two sales, one pending. A storm-restoration contractor with a four-year relationship produces this page with thirty rows in it and no way to collapse them into the four jobs they actually represent.

AND THE HEADER IS WRONG

The page is titled with an address, and the object is called a lead. Sara Thompson has bought twice over two years. She has been a customer for longer than she was a lead, and the product has never stopped calling her one.

THE CONTAINER IS THE WHOLE FIX

Nothing on this page is a new interaction. It is the same objects with one level inserted above them, so that work sold together can be grouped and work delivered separately can still be separate.

That single insertion is what produces the total owed, the single loan, the single contract, and the two jobs that are visibly part of one thing.

THE SIMPLE CASE DID NOT GET HARDER

Gutter replacement is a project with one opportunity, one proposal and one job. It renders as one line. A contractor who never bundles never meets the concept, which is the test any version of this has to pass.

THE CHANGE ORDER NOW SAYS WHAT IT WAS ABOUT

Attached to the siding scope rather than to a financing record, so it carries both the reason and the money. The re-underwriting still happens; it is just no longer the only thing recorded.

WHAT THIS SCREEN IS NOT ARGUING

Whether the project holds scopes inside one proposal or gathers several whole proposals. That is the open question, it is argued in its own memo, and this page looks the same either way.

1

Does a project need a name a human writes, or is one generated from its opportunities good enough? A named project is a small tax on every sale.

2

Should the deferred roofing opportunity sit on the customer, as drawn, or inside the project it was deferred out of?

3

Is a rolled-up total across three projects, one of them four years closed, actually useful, or is it a number nobody asks for?

C

What is actually in the codebase

DIAGNOSIS

Today's shape · everything of consequence hangs off one object

FindingWhat the code doesConsequence
The proposal hangs off a lead, and a lead is an address Proposals are keyed by leadId. There is no person-level identity above the property. One customer with two properties, or two decision-makers at one property, has nowhere to live. This is the largest structural change of the four.
Financing is proposal-level and singular in practice The financing bundles field is an array, but each bundle is a set of quotes, quotes are cash or loan, and the code works with a single active bundle selected by id. The array reads as alternatives offered, not several financings running at once. Two proposals sold in one conversation therefore cannot share a loan, because there is nothing above the proposal for one to attach to.
A change order is a financing change, not a scope change It fires when the loan amount, offer, autopay election or cash price differs from the active bundle, and resolves by updating the proposal's financing. Blocked outright when the loan is conditional. There is already machinery for "the money changed after the customer said yes", attached to the proposal. There is nothing attaching a change order to a piece of work.
The existing "projects" concept is a design artefact, not a commercial one On the proposal it is a version pointer. Resolved, it carries a bill of materials, an electricity profile and a design. It is closer to "the engineered system" than to "the priced chunk of scope". It is the nearest existing thing to what we need and still not the same thing.
A proposal holds exactly one scope design context Asserted in the model work rather than read out of the code: there is no additive subdivision layer inside a proposal. Worth confirming before it is relied on. Bundling is either impossible or done by convention outside the data. This is the extension the open question is about.

THESE CHANGE WHICH OPTIONS EXIST

They are stated first because they are constraints, not colour. Two of them make a design option cheaper than it looks and two make one more expensive, and it is not obvious in advance which is which without reading them.

THE GOOD NEWS IS THE CHANGE ORDER

The hard part, re-underwriting a loan after acceptance, is already built. It is bounded rather than free: blocked outright when the loan is conditional, and held inside the category’s minimum and maximum. What is missing is only the link from the money to the work it was about, which is a smaller ask than it sounds from the outside.

THE BAD NEWS IS THE LEAD

Person-above-address is not an addition, it is a re-parenting. Everything currently keyed to a lead has to acquire an owner. It is tracked separately from this work for that reason, but no version of the model works without it.

A WARNING ABOUT THE WORD PROJECT

Model C wants to call its container a project. Merlin already has a project and it means the engineered system. That collision has to be resolved with engineering early rather than discovered in review.

1

Is the existing project concept closer to a priced chunk of scope than I have read it? If so, question one of the open decisions turns from new work into an extension.

2

Is a join table between opportunities and scopes acceptable, or is there pressure to keep a single foreign key? I would rather know the constraint than design around one that is not there.

3

Is person-above-address genuinely on someone's roadmap, or is it a dependency that nobody currently owns?

D

The vocabulary is wrong too, and that is not cosmetic

DIAGNOSIS
WordThe problemWhat it costs
Lead A permanent name for a temporary condition. A lead is the first thirty seconds of a relationship; in Merlin it is the object that persists for its whole life. A homeowner who has bought three times over four years is still, structurally and on screen, a lead. The contractor's vocabulary and the product's vocabulary diverge on day one and never reconverge. Every conversation about the record needs a translation step.
Project Already taken. In Merlin it is the engineered system: a version pointer carrying a bill of materials, an electricity profile and a design. Model C wants the same word for the commercial container. Two meanings in one codebase and one UI. Either Model C picks another word or the existing one is renamed, and the second is more work than it sounds.
Scope Collides with itself. Model C uses scope for a priced, additive unit of a proposal. The insurance sub-status uses "Scope received" for the carrier's damage document. Both appear on the same job on the same day. A contractor doing insurance restoration reads the word twice on one screen meaning two different things. One of them has to be renamed before either ships.
Opportunity Not a collision, an absence. There is no word because there is no object, so the thing a rep spots on a service visit has no name and no home. Pipeline is only what has already been priced. Anything noticed and not yet quoted falls out of the system entirely.

NAMING IS THE CHEAPEST PART TO FIX AND THE MOST EXPENSIVE TO LEAVE

These are not polish items to be handled at the end. Two of the four are collisions, which means every conversation between design and engineering about the model silently carries an ambiguity, and the ambiguity survives into the code.

"LEAD" IS THE ONE THE CONTRACTOR NOTICES

The other three are internal. Lead is on the screen, in the navigation, and in the mouth of every rep who has to explain why the system calls their best repeat customer a lead.

WHERE I COULD BE WRONG

It is possible the industry has simply accepted "lead" as a permanent record name and nobody blinks. Several CRMs do exactly this. If field research says contractors do not notice, this item drops to a nice-to-have and the memo is weaker for it.

1

Do we rename the existing project concept, or does Model C give up the word? Deciding late is much worse than deciding either way now.

2

Which "scope" gets renamed? The insurance one is inherited industry vocabulary, which argues for keeping it and renaming ours.

3

Does anyone outside design object to retiring "lead" as a persistent object name, or is the resistance purely migration cost?
