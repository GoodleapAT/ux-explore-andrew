One project, start to finish

Eight moments in one sale, drawn as screens rather than diagrams. Northgate Home Services sells siding and windows to the Thompsons, defers a roof, finds rot at tear-off, and gets held at closeout by a permit inspection. The point is to see whether the model survives being looked at.

[i] Inside a mockup, small grey text prefixed with [i] is my annotation, not interface copy. Everything else in a mockup frame is intended as real UI.
Taken as settled for this memoCarried in from the Model C memo ▸
  • A project is always mandatory and is the container. Opportunities, proposals and jobs are peers inside it.
  • The project has two phases. Selling holds property details, notes and a proposal. Operations arrives on winning and brings the money and the jobs.
  • One acceptance, one contract, one financing. A proposal holds scopes; one job per signed scope.
  • Opportunities link into a project and keep their provenance. Status is what moves.
  • Jobs run stages, tasks, sub-statuses and gates. A gate sits on a transition, not on a stage.
Invented for this memo, overrule freelyThree things nothing else specifies ▸

Project status vocabulary. Pursuing, production, closing, closed, with archived for a project that never sells. Nothing we have written defines one.

Payment milestones. Twenty percent deposit, forty on install start, forty on completion. Every money figure in these screens follows from that.

The change order subject. Rot behind the siding, found at tear-off. The old deck rot example belonged to a roofing job, and roofing was deferred.

The project is the verb

Nothing here is a project yet, and the action that creates one is selecting opportunities and starting. That single control is where the model becomes visible to a contractor, and it is the only place a project is ever born.

Two empty states, not one

No projects and nothing owed are different absences. Collapsing them into one "nothing here yet" would hide that this customer has been engaged and measured, just not sold to.

Estimates before pricing

Rough figures on unpursued opportunities, deliberately round. They are what makes a list of three worth triaging, and they must not be confused with a scope price.

Only one block here is new

This adapts the proposal page that already exists. Financing, presentation and accept are broadly what is there today. Scopes are the addition, and a scope has to be independently priceable and removable live, in front of a customer. If it cannot be toggled without re-authoring the document, Model C's fork tilts toward the folio.

One document or one at a time

The presentation control sits in the header, not in the body, because it changes how the same proposal is walked through rather than what it contains. One signature either way.

Declining is recorded, not erased

Roofing stays visible with its price, switched off. That is what feeds the deferred status back to the opportunity and gives a follow-up in two years something to stand on.

Nothing was replaced

The selling phase is still here with all three proposal versions. Winning adds a half rather than closing one, which is what lets someone six weeks in ask what was originally quoted and why roofing went away.

Two jobs, not one job with two halves

Each has a crew, a value and its own pipeline position. They will diverge within days, and the screen has to be able to show them at different stages without apology.

The money is at project level

One master invoice against the contract, not one per job. Job values sum to it, but no job owns an invoice, which is what keeps the balance answerable in one place.

The change order is a loop inside one stage

A task found the rot, the change order came out of it, and now it blocks a different task in the same stage. Putting it beside the task list rather than in a paperwork section is what makes that legible.

Two numbers, because two things move

The scope goes to $37,000 and the master invoice to $55,600. Showing one without the other hides which figure triggers re-approval, and it is never the scope.

The sibling job is one line

Enough to know windows has not slipped, not enough to distract. The full picture of both jobs belongs on the project, and this is the link to it.

A gate that costs money is the honest test

Every earlier drawing had gates blocking work. This one blocks an invoice worth $23,920, which is where a contractor finds out whether they believe in the gate. If this is the one they override every time, it should not exist.

The project status is not derived

One job is in closeout and the other is closed. Closing is the project's own word for the situation, not a summary of its jobs, which is why the vocabularies have to be separate.

The money reads as a schedule

Two paid, one held, with the reason attached to the held one. That is more useful than a balance, because it says what has to happen next rather than only how much is left.
