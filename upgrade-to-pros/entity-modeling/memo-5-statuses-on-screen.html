Where the statuses live

The model is settled enough to draw. This memo tests one thing: whether the status sets agreed for leads, projects, proposals and jobs survive being put on screen. Four screens follow the Thompsons through a two trade sale with a deferred roof and a permit that sticks. A fifth screen is the service plan on their heat pump, which needs a different set of statuses and is the reason this memo has five sections rather than four.

[i] Inside a mockup, small grey text prefixed with [i] is my annotation, not interface copy. Everything else in a mockup frame is intended as real UI.

Taken as settled for this memo

Working assumptions from the DRAFT v3 comparison, not decisions anyone has signed off

  • The project is the spine, and it mints on pursuit. It is created when someone starts working a lead with the intention of writing a proposal, so it exists before anything is priced.
  • One lead per trade. Three trades on one house are three leads, and they can be pursued into a single project.
  • Statuses are authored on leads, proposals and jobs, and derived on the project. The only project status a person sets is the first one.
  • Deferred is not lost. A declined trade keeps its lead open with a reason, a note and a revive date, and keeps its priced option on the proposal.
  • Every unhappy state carries a reason code and a free text note, written for whoever picks the conversation up next season.
  • A proposal carries two statuses. A lifecycle one and a delivery one, because revised and unsent are two different facts.
  • Selling and work are groupings in the experience, not entities. They exist so a person can see the line between what is being sold and what is being delivered.
  • Service runs as its own project type, with one job per visit and a cadence taken from the plan terms.

Invented for this memo, overrule freely

Three things nothing we have written specifies

  • The permit stall. Windows needs a building permit, plan review returns a correction on 10 September, and nothing about the job is wrong. Taken from the financed multi trade scenario, because it is the only place a status has to survive four weeks of silence.
  • The heat pump service plan. Sold at closeout of the 2025 HVAC job, two visits a year at twenty nine dollars a month. Nothing in the model says a plan is sold this way; it is the flywheel drawn as a fact so section E has something to stand on.
  • Reason code vocabularies. The specific codes shown on the deferred roof and the blocked job are made up. The claim is that codes exist and are required, not that these are the right ones.

Deferred needs a date, or it rots

A status alone does not make anything happen. The revive date is what turns the roof from a note somebody wrote into a thing that appears in a queue next March. Without it, deferred and lost behave identically no matter what the reporting says.

The reason is the deliverable

The code drives reporting and the free text drives the next conversation. Both were captured in the room on 18 August, which is the only moment anyone knows why. Making the note optional would be the same as not having it.

Won leads stay on the list

Siding and windows read Won rather than disappearing into the project. It costs a longer list, and it buys the only honest answer to "what have we sold these people", which is the question this screen exists for.

Counts differ at every level

Three leads, one project, two jobs, one plan. The screen has to hold four different numbers without implying they should match, which is why leads and projects are separate lists rather than a tree.

The uncomfortable part

The roofing lead reads Deferred while the project holding it reads Sold. On this screen two statuses appear to disagree and both are correct, because one is about demand and the other is about commitment. That is the cost of keeping them separate, and it is the first item in the to decide list at the end.

A derived status has to show its working

If a person cannot set the status, they will not trust it until they can see what produced it. The one line explanation under the header is doing more work than the pill above it, and it is the difference between a computed field and a field people quietly work around.

A blocked job says what blocks it

In the financed walkthrough the windows job sat blocked for twenty six days with nothing in its own record saying so, because the blocking fact lived on the permit. Deriving Blocked from the blocker, and showing the elapsed days, is the fix and it costs one pointer.

Two lanes, one status, no averaging

Siding is running and windows is stuck. The project reads In progress off the first and does not soften it with the second. Any status that tried to describe both at once would describe neither.

The declined trade stays inside Selling

Roofing sits under the proposal it was priced on, greyed, linked back to its lead. That is the DRAFT v3 idea worth taking: the demand survives on the lead, and the number survives here.

The uncomfortable part

Per job margin is not answerable and the screen says so out loud. Every model in play has this gap, because revenue attaches one level above where costs land. Showing a blank is better than showing an allocation nobody agreed.

Two axes because they move independently

Revised and unsent are two facts about the same document, and you asked for the second without losing the first. A single list would force a state called something like revised, not sent, which then has to be duplicated for every lifecycle state it can co‑occur with.

No partially accepted state

Two trades are taken and one is declined, and the proposal still reads Accepted. The per trade decision lives on the option, which is where DRAFT v3 already puts it. A compound proposal status would have to encode the mix and would be unreportable.

Held back is a presentation state, not a draft

Windows was fully priced and deliberately not shown. That is a different thing from an unfinished line, and the inversion where the held back trade wins and the offered one loses is what makes the distinction worth paying for.

Switching off is gated on a reason

The reason and note are required to turn an offered option off. That is friction in the highest pressure moment of the sale, deliberately, because it is the only moment the answer exists. It will be unpopular.

Outer states fixed, steps configured

This is the line that keeps the model safe. The six outer states are what the project's derived status reads, so they cannot be renamed by an org. Everything an org wants to rename sits underneath them as steps.

Blocked is derived, and it clears itself

Nobody sets Blocked and nobody has to remember to unset it. It comes from a live blocker record with a pointer back, and it disappears when the permit issues. A manually set block is a block that stays on after the problem goes away.

The clock does the work the status cannot

Blocked is a fact. Fourteen days is a prompt. In the scenario the job waited twenty six days with nothing in its record saying anything at all, which is the single most expensive gap the walkthroughs found.

Completion is evidence, not a button

Three pieces of evidence, one of them a municipal signature. Deriving Complete means nobody can close a job to clear their queue, and it means the final bill cannot be issued early.

The uncomfortable part

If one permit ever covers both siding and windows, the same blocker has to hold two jobs and the evidence has to be shared. Nothing on this screen handles that, and the scenario set flags it as unresolved.

The terminal states have to differ

A one off project ends at Paid because a balance closes. A plan has no balance, so the run from Sold to Paid has nothing to describe. Forcing this project type through the same set would leave every service plan permanently unfinished.

Upcoming is a status one off work does not need

The March 2027 visit exists because the cadence says so, before anybody schedules it and before the renewal is sold. That is the mechanical difference between work produced by a signed scope and work produced by a term.

Missed and Skipped decide money

Whether a visit consumed an entitlement is not an operational detail, it is what the homeowner paid for. Complete, Missed and Skipped have to be distinguishable, and only one of them decrements the count.

Suspended is not Cancelled

When the card fails and dunning runs out, the plan suspends: the cadence stops firing and no visit job mints, but nothing is deleted and the installed equipment keeps its registration. A suspended plan can come back and a cancelled one starts over.

The uncomfortable part

The monthly invoice has no valid parent. Money in this model hangs off a job or a project, and a subscription charge belongs to neither. Drawing it as unresolved is honest; it is also the thing most likely to force a change above the container.

To decide

Three things this memo could not avoid and did not settle. The first two are questions about the model. The third is a question about the screens, and every mockup above dodged it with a labelled placeholder.

01

Whether a deferred lead holds its project back

The Thompsons committed two trades and deferred one. The project reads Sold, and one of the leads that fed it reads Deferred. If project status derives from its leads, a deferred sibling could stop the project ever reading Sold, and the more leads a project carries the worse it gets.

Drawn here as: project status derives from the proposal and the jobs, never from the leads. A lead's status is about demand and the project's is about the commitment, and the two are allowed to disagree.

If this is wrong, the model is wrong rather than the layout. It would mean either that a project cannot carry a lead it did not sell, which breaks bundling, or that lead status and project status are the same fact recorded twice.

02

Where the configured pipeline surfaces

Stages, tasks, sub statuses and gates are cross cutting, which is why the model deliberately does not draw them at any one level. But a screen has to put them somewhere. Section D shows steps on the job, and sections A and B both leave a placeholder where a pipeline would go.

The live tension is that the ten configured stages an org sets up describe the whole run from first contact to closeout, and that run crosses the lead, the project, selling and the jobs. Whoever configures it thinks of it as one pipeline. The model has it as four.

Cost of deciding late: every screen in this memo has an empty region in it, and the next round cannot lay any of them out properly.

03

Where documents, notes, measurements and history attach

They hang off the customer, the project, the proposal, the jobs and the money at once. Putting them on one object asserts an ownership the model is not claiming, and putting them everywhere means five places to look for the same photograph.

Section A has the placeholder at customer level and section D shows checklists on the job. Neither is a position, and the site assessment from 20 August is the awkward case: one record, findings tagged per trade, later read by two jobs that never run in step.

Cost of deciding late: lower than the other two. This can be settled per screen in the next round as long as nobody treats the first answer as a rule.
