Taken out, and things needing a home

The previous version of this page had a status rail and three explanatory boxes. All of it is here instead.

Taken out

The derived status rail. Seven states in a bordered box, grouped by what produced them. It was a diagram of the model rather than a thing anyone needs on a Tuesday, and In progress in the meta line says as much.

Taken out

The blue explanation box. In progress because one of two jobs has started. It becomes Complete when both jobs are complete, and Paid when the balance clears. Suggested as a tooltip on the status word, or as nothing at all.

Taken out

Money footnote. One master invoice for the whole project. Both jobs draw against it, and each job's evidence releases its own milestone. True, and only interesting the first time.

Taken out

Per job margin, shown as unavailable. Naming a gap in the UI invites someone to fill it badly. The gap is real and belongs in the model notes, not the card.

Needs a home

The configured pipeline. Ten stages describing first contact to closeout, crossing the lead, the project and the jobs. It is a placeholder here because putting it in the project card and in the job card would be the same pipeline twice.

Question

Leads on a project page. The only card here showing something the project does not own. It earns its place because the deferred roof would otherwise be invisible from inside the project that failed to sell it.

Question

Selling and Work as headings. Written as Selling and Work rather than Sales and Operations, because the line is what is being sold against what is being delivered, not which team is doing it.

Question

Contract and change orders under Proposals. They are not proposals. Grouping them there keeps the selling side to two cards; the alternative is a third card and a thinner row.

This is the one page where the rail belongs, because these are the contractor's own stages rather than the model's states.

Taken out

The stage pill in the header. Blocked is now the first word of the meta line, in ink. The rail says which stage, and the amber strip says why nothing is moving.

Taken out

The collapsed gate notice. It was a second warning box under the rail, competing with the one at the top. Now a single line in the card footer, and the gate list is a click away.

Taken out

Five sub‑status registers reduced to four. Insurance was never applicable on a retail job, and a permanently not applicable row is furniture.

Suggested copy

On the blocked task, on hover: this task cannot be completed by anyone here. The city holds it. The distinction between late and blocked is the one thing a crew lead needs and cannot infer.

Needs a home

Days blocked, as a number the job owns. Fourteen days is computed from the permit's application date and appears in the strip. Nothing in the job's own record carries it, so a list of jobs cannot sort by it.

Needs a home

The sibling. Siding is running, on the same contract and the same loan, and this page never mentions it. Scope 2 of 2 in the meta line is the only hint that a sibling exists.

Question

A permit card at stage level. Drawn as the third card in the current stage because that is the stage it blocks. When the permit is approved the card has nothing to say and the row of three becomes a row of two.

Question

One permit covering two jobs. Not the case here. If it were, this card would have to appear on both jobs and the evidence would have to be shared, which nothing in the model handles.

Taken out, and things needing a home

The most speculative of the five. Almost nothing about services is settled in the model.

Taken out

A closing balance row. A plan never closes out, so the billing card has no balance due and no total. The absence is the design, and it is the clearest way the page differs from a project.

Taken out

Status pills on visits. Complete, Scheduled and Upcoming are now the right hand column. The dots on the left carry past, present and future without repeating it in colour.

Suggested copy

Under the visits card: the March 2027 visit exists because the cadence says so, not because anyone booked it. Worth saying once, because no other job in the product behaves this way.

Suggested copy

Under the entitlement row: a missed visit and a skipped visit are different. Only one of them uses up an entitlement. That distinction is not visible anywhere on this page because neither has happened.

Needs a home

What the monthly invoice belongs to. Money in the model hangs off a job or a project, and a subscription charge belongs to neither. This page shows the charges without saying what their parent is, because there is no answer yet.

Needs a home

Where the visits live. Drawn as jobs directly under the plan. The alternative is a project per visit cycle, which is what the scenario set implies and would put a container between this page and every job on it.

Question

The condenser fan. A repair found on a maintenance visit is new demand. Shown here as a watch row with a raise a lead action, which turns a service page into a selling surface.

Question

Transfer on a house sale. The plan is written against the customer and the property, and those come apart when the Thompsons move. Nothing on this page or in the model handles it.
