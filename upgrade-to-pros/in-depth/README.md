# In-depth documents

**One question, worked through, then it stops changing.**

> **Who may write here:** any surface that can write. **One document per question.** Name it after the
> question, not after the answer, because the answer changes while it is open and the question does
> not.

## What belongs here, and what does not

The sorting rule is whether the file is ever finished.

| | Lives here | Lives in the project folder |
|---|---|---|
| **Shape** | A worked argument that reaches a conclusion | A register you add rows to |
| **Finished?** | Yes, eventually. Then it is a record of reasoning | Never |
| **Examples** | How a lead becomes a project. What a service plan is. Where the pipeline surfaces | Sources, decisions, deviations, the object register, the worked example, the catalogue |

A memo or a mockup is neither of these. Those are artefacts and they live with the thread that made
them.

## Every document carries a header

Four things, so somebody opening it knows what they have before reading:

- **The question**, in one line.
- **Status:** open, or concluded on a date.
- **What it settled**, listed, with each item marked as gone to the decision log or not.
- **What it did not settle**, listed.

## The rule that makes this worth doing

**When a document concludes, its settlements go to the decision log and the document stays as the
reasoning.**

The log holds **what**. The document holds **why**. Without the split, one of two things happens:
either the decisions stay buried in prose that nobody rereads, or somebody summarises the prose into
a row and the reasoning is lost the moment anybody asks why.

A document can conclude in parts. Settle five things, log them, leave four open, keep going.

## When a document is overtaken rather than concluded

**Added 14 Sep 2026, after it happened twice in one day.** A document can be finished by its own
reasoning, which is what concluding means. It can also be finished by somebody else answering its
question, which is different and should look different.

**The rule: say which one happened, and do not tidy the record.** An overtaken document keeps its
original text, gains a box at the top saying what overtook it and when, and names its replacement if
there is one. If the premise then moves again, **the box gains a line rather than being rewritten**,
because the sequence of changes is usually more informative than any one of them.

**And an overtaken document must not restate the position that overtook it.** Point at the row in
`../TEAM-POSITIONS.md`. Otherwise the document goes stale a second time, which is exactly what
happened to the first beats document.
