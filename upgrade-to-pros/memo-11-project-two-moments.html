The same page when everything is ahead of you, and when nothing is left to do

Two states of one container, chosen because they invert the page's job. At the first, ten things must happen on two jobs and none has started. At the second, all ten are done and the only question is whether Monday will go wrong.

Seven groups, in the same order at both. Nothing moves up the page. What changes is how much of each group is showing, and one group collapses.

Settled, for the purposes of this memo

From the data pack and yesterday's conversation.

  • Five ops steps are tasks. Two things are sub-statuses. Permit and materials are external waits, shown as pills, never as checklist rows.
  • Money is a pair, Balance and Revenue and profit, lifted from the customer record work and reused here at a different level.
  • The post-sale inspection is one per project, not one per job. One property, one visit, two trades, as the sales assessment was.
  • Provenance collapses after the hand-off, and the asset index does not.

To decide, and it is the one that would change both screens

Everything else here is arrangeable.

  • Where does a project-level task attach? The post-sale inspection covers both jobs, and the project carries no task list by design. It is drawn on the project as a single row above the two job cards, which is a claim the model does not currently support.
  • If the answer is that it cannot, the inspection becomes two tasks, one per job, booked on the same visit, and the count on each card goes from five to five with a duplicate.

The argument

  • The page has one live action and ten dormant ones. Book the inspection is the only button, because every other step is behind it. The remaining eight rows are visible and quiet, which is what a checklist is for.
  • The third tile counts set-up, not money. Two money tiles and one work tile, because at this moment the work is the question and the money is settled.

Needs a home

  • The inspection row sits on the project and the model has nowhere to put it. The project carries no task list by design. This is the to-decide card.
  • "0 of 10" totals two jobs' checklists. A derived figure across siblings, which nothing in the model computes.

Question

  • Should Revenue and profit appear before any work has happened? It is entirely forecast, and a forecast margin on the day of signing may be more confidence than the number deserves.
  • Does Activity earn a group this early, with two rows in it?

The argument

  • No buttons anywhere. Ten of ten done, nothing due, nothing waiting. A page with no action on it is a real state and the scenario produces it twice.
  • The schedule is the only group that gained weight, and it earns it by being the one thing that answers the question Marcus came with.
  • Provenance collapsed to one line and nothing moved up the page to fill the space. That is the order rule doing its job.

Needs a home

  • The day-before checks have no beat and no rows. Weather, access, dumpster, customer notified. The configuration has a stage for this and the script does not.
  • The schedule bars are the first thing drawn on a time axis, and nothing in the model says a job has a duration rather than a start and an end.

Question

  • Money is identical to screen A and I did not collapse it. It has not moved in eight days, which argues for a line. But collapsing on recency is a different rule from collapsing on stage, and mixing them is what made the last memo wander.
  • Is "Ready. Roofing starts Monday" a state, or a sentence pretending to be one?

Three things to settle, in the order they cost

Where a project-level task attaches. The post-sale inspection covers both trades in one visit and is drawn on the project above the job cards. The project carries no task list by design, so either that changes or the inspection becomes two tasks booked on one visit.

Whether Revenue and profit belongs on a project before any work has happened. Both screens show a forecast margin of 31% derived from the script's own figure, and on screen A that is the day the contract was signed.

Whether money collapses when it has not moved. I left it full at both states and said so rather than deciding quietly. Collapsing on recency and collapsing on stage are two different rules, and the stage table cannot hold both.
