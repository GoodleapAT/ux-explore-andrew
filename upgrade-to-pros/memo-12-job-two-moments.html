What a job page has to say before anyone has done anything, and once everything is booked

One job, the roofing half of the Thompson sale, at the two moments where its own page changes most. At the first it has just been created and nothing on it is true yet. At the second every arrangement is made and the only thing left is for Monday to arrive.

Seven groups, in the same order at both. Nothing moves up the page. What this memo is really testing is whether a job page earns its existence beside the project page, or whether it is the project page with fewer rows.

Settled, for the purposes of this memo

Carried in from the data pack, the pipeline documents and memo 11.

  • The job is drawn on the contractor's configured stages. Both pipeline documents say do not act on them, so this is a declared departure. The stage and task names are Dana's Roofing configuration and nobody has agreed them. Two are reworded for the screen: BOM finalised reads Materials list finalised, and scope is moved to the top of the list.
  • The job does not hold money. Revenue occurs at the project and cost at the job, so the page names its price and points upward for the balance.
  • Per-job margin and per-job cost are absent. Not greyed, not labelled, not explained on screen.
  • Permit and materials are sub-statuses, not tasks. A thing the contractor can move is a task; a thing they can only wait for is a sub-status.
  • There is no job number. The job is called Roofing.

To decide, and it decides whether this page exists

Layout is arrangeable. This is not.

  • Whose stages are these? The rail is the only thing on this page that a project page could not also carry. It comes from a configuration document that says it is a sketch, while the model's own job vocabulary has six words and no stages at all.
  • If the model is right, the rail comes off and the job page has almost nothing left to say about progress. That is a failure of the model rather than of the layout, and it would collapse the job into a section of the project page.
  • A second, smaller one that shows up in both mocks: the permit value set has no word for required but not yet applied. The values run Not required, Applied, Approved, Final inspection passed. On 6 August the roofing permit is none of those.

The rail is the reason the page exists

Strip it out and what is left is the project page with one trade in it. The rail is the only element here that belongs to a job and could not sit on the project, because the project deliberately carries no stages. That makes it both the most valuable thing on the page and the least defensible, since its ten stage names come from a document that says it is a sketch.

Waiting on is empty at birth, and that is the finding

On 6 August nothing external is holding this job. The permit is not filed and the materials are not ordered, and both of those are Marcus's own work, which makes them tasks rather than waits. So the card is drawn as a single line rather than a card with rows, because a full card listing things nobody is waiting for says nothing.

A price, not a balance

The money group keeps its heading and holds one card, which is a sentence and four facts rather than the money card used on a customer or a project. The rule that revenue occurs at the project is what makes a balance meaningless here, and the honest move is to name the price, name where the balance lives, and link to it.

In review, not Not started

The ratified job vocabulary runs Review, Ready, In progress, Complete, Blocked, Cancelled. A job at the hand-off is in review because its scope has not been checked against what was sold, which is the first task in the stage. Memo 11 used "Not started" on the project page, and that word is not in the set.

One button, and it is the next task

Refine the scope is the first thing that happens on this job, on 7 August. Everything else on the page is either a fact or a placeholder. Giving the page one action makes the next move obvious without a checklist telling anybody what to do, and it is the only emphasis on an otherwise colourless screen.

What is deliberately thin

Crew, dates and the material order are empty states rather than absent groups, because they will fill within six days and a group that disappears and comes back is harder to hold than a group that fills. The cost is three cards that say nothing on a page that is already short of content.

Jobs to be done, and what on the page serves them

  • J-06 Hand a signed sale to the people who will deliver it. This screen is the whole of J-06. The scope card, the property and access card and the provenance list are the translation from what was sold into what somebody can start, and the sibling card is what stops the second trade being forgotten.
  • J-05 Find out what is actually stopping a job. Served by answering nothing. The waiting-on line says so in one line rather than making somebody read four settled rows to find out.
  • J-04 Know what is owed and what has been collected. Deliberately not served here. The money group answers it by pointing at the project, which is the correct answer and a worse experience than answering it.
  • J-07 Walk into a conversation knowing what was said last time. Partly served by notes and assets. Communications have no entity, so the call on 4 August is a note and not a record of a call.

Open questions

  1. The rail runs Ops intake through Closeout, but Closeout sits on a board that shows projects, not jobs. Does a job's rail cross a board boundary, or does it stop at Installing and hand over?
  2. What does the permit sub-status read on 6 August? Not required is false and Applied is not yet true. The value set is missing a word, and the mock ducks it by not showing a permit pill at all.
  3. Ops intake reads 0 of 3 with a fourth row marked not applicable. Should the count include it? Three of four is honest about the configuration; three is honest about the work.
  4. The scope card says "not yet refined". Is that a state of the scope or a task that has not happened? Drawing it as both, which is what this does, is how the same fact ends up in two places.
  5. Should the sibling card sit at the bottom or beside the stage rail? It is the thing most likely to change what Marcus does next, and it is currently last.

Same seven groups, and one of them collapses

Every group is where it was on screen A. Only the provenance card collapses, from four rows to one line, because after the work is booked it is history rather than the thing you came for. Nothing moved up the page to take the space, which is the layout rule doing what it is there for.

No buttons at all

Six of six is done, the permit is approved, the gate is satisfied and the materials have a date. There is nothing for anybody to do on this page until Sunday, so there is no action and no warning colour. A page that offers a button here would be inventing work.

The gate is satisfied the day before it matters

Permits were filed on 7 August and approved on 11 August, and the crew was booked on the 12th. The one live gate in this whole scenario cleared with a day to spare, which means the rail's footer never actually stopped anybody. It is worth showing precisely because that is the happy case and it is what the footer will look like most of the time.

Materials is the only thing anybody is waiting on

The waiting-on line now has exactly one thing in it, and the delivery is three days before the crew. One line carries that better than a card with one row and three settled ones, and it means the line's content, rather than its presence, is what tells you whether to worry.

The week track shows the sibling, not just this job

The dates card draws both trades across one week, with siding quiet. The constraint that matters on 12 August is that two crews are at one address in one week, and a bar showing only roofing would hide it. This is the first thing in the memo series drawn on a time axis, and nothing in the model says a job has a duration.

What the page still cannot say

There is no cost on this job and therefore no margin, and the page does not mention either. The absence is deliberate and is not marked, because naming a gap invites somebody to fill it badly. The honest discomfort is that a production coordinator looking at a $38,900 job cannot see what it will cost to deliver.

Jobs to be done, and what on the page serves them

  • J-05 Find out what is actually stopping a job. This screen is the clean answer: one line naming the materials, and a gate footer naming the permit and the date it cleared. Nothing else on the page claims to be a blocker.
  • J-06 Hand a signed sale to the people who will deliver it. Completed rather than served. The scope, access, crew and dates together are the handover finished, and the page proves it with dates rather than assertions.
  • J-08 Get paid the moment the work earns it. Not served, and cannot be from here. The trigger is both jobs complete, which is a project fact, so the money group points upward at both moments.
  • J-07 Walk into a conversation knowing what was said last time. The two notes from the 12 August call are the only record of it. The call itself has no entity, so what a reader sees is somebody's summary rather than the conversation.

Open questions

  1. Final checks shows a date it was never given. The scenario has no day-before beat, so Sun 16 Aug is derived from the install date. Should a rail predict, or only report?
  2. The materials delivery is on 14 August and the crew arrives on the 17th. Is a confirmed delivery a wait at all, or does waiting-on only earn its place when a date is missing or at risk?
  3. Money is identical to screen A and is not collapsed. Collapsing because it has not moved in six days is a different rule from collapsing because of the stage, and the page cannot hold both.
  4. The week track is the second place the schedule appears, after the tile that reads Mon 17 Aug. Is the tile redundant once the track exists, or is it the answer for somebody who reads nothing else?
  5. Both jobs read 6 of 6 and Ready at the same moment. If the sibling card is always the same as this job, it is decoration, and it only earns its place on 18 August when roofing is in closeout and siding has not started.

Where this leaves the four things

Carried over as is

  • Seven groups, fixed order, a group that is not needed collapsing in place.
  • Two cards abreast, and a lone card keeping its group heading.
  • No reference codes. No job, permit or invoice numbers anywhere.
  • Status in the second line of the right-hand column, under the value.

New here

  • The stage rail on a job, with board names as headers and the gate in the footer.
  • Waiting on as a line rather than a card, which is the component record's own recommendation, tested.
  • A money group that holds a price and a pointer instead of a balance.
  • A week track carrying both trades, so the sibling constraint is visible.

Deliberately refused

  • Per-job cost and margin. Absent, not greyed, not explained.
  • Roofing line items. None exist in the data pack, and inventing them would be invention twice over.
  • An inspection. S15 has none, and the post-sale inspection on memo 11 is a memo invention.
  • A what changed link on the refined scope. The model does not keep the earlier version.

Placeholder for now

  • Documents and photos counts. Twelve assessment photos is ours, not the script's.
  • The dumpster. Drawn as a material order row, which is a guess at where it belongs.
  • Final checks on 16 August. Derived from the install date, not given.
  • Activity. Not drawn at all, because two rows do not earn a group this early.
