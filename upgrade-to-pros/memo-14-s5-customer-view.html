The same four groups as S15, on a record that holds one address, one payment and four words

Ten months ago Caldwell Brothers took a card payment on a porch. Today Dana Whitfield wants a gate. This is the record Wes lands on, built on the shell and the group order you arrived at in Claude Design for S15.

The S15 memos tested a page with too much on it. This tests the other end of the same template, and the question underneath is whether a near-empty record is worth opening at all, or whether Wes should have started the payment from the list.

Settled, for the purposes of this memo

From the two files you sent, the team's S5 flow map, and today's conversation.

  • Dana Whitfield is the homeowner. The contractor is Caldwell Brothers Fence and Deck, two brothers and four in season, and Wes Caldwell is the one in the app. This Dana has nothing to do with Dana's Roofing in the pipeline document.
  • The shell is yours, not invented. Sidebar at 256px, nav items and order, account row at the foot, page as a white panel inside a grey shell, all taken from the customer list you sent.
  • Four groups in the same order at every state: Overview, Active projects, Selling, Money. Two of them have nothing in them at this tier, so they disappear rather than render empty, which leaves Overview and Money.
  • The previous project is a row inside Overview, under Projects and service plans, which is where your S15 record puts it rather than in a group of its own.
  • No contact detail was ever asked for. The name and address were typed on a porch and the receipt went to a number that lives on the transaction, not on a contact.

To decide, and neither is a layout question

  • Wes needs to call Dana about the gate, and her number is on a card receipt from last June. Surfacing it promotes rail metadata to a contact, which the model says it is not. Not surfacing it means opening a ten month old transaction to find your own customer's number. Drawn here surfaced, with its provenance in the sub line, which is a claim the model does not support.
  • Money is last, and at this tier it is the only group with anything in it. Keeping your S15 order means the page opens on a near-empty Overview. The composition rules say a payments-only contractor sees financials first, and those two rules disagree. Drawn your way, because one order across all three tiers is worth more than a better order at one of them.

Two groups instead of four

Active projects and Selling carry nothing at this tier, so they are gone rather than empty. A group that is not needed disappears from where it was, which is the same rule the S15 pair runs on, and it is what keeps one order working across three tiers instead of three orders.

Two ways to start a payment, on purpose

The button sits in the money group and the same action sits at the top of the overflow menu. Taking a payment is the entire product at this tier, so the button is the working route and the menu is where somebody looks who does not yet know where the button is. On a fuller tier one of the two is redundant.

The action tier's rule has to bend

The money card says at most one button, and only when somebody must act. Nothing is owed here and nothing is overdue. At a payments-only tier the condition changes from somebody must act to this is the one thing the product does, and that belongs in the component record as an amendment rather than as a quiet departure.

Four words carry the whole job

The charge was amount-only, so there is no scope, no line items and no estimate. The memo is the only description that has ever existed of this work. It is doing the job of a project name, a job name and a statement of work at once, and the row shows it as typed rather than tidied.

One contact, and it is half a contact

The name came from a porch and the phone number came off a card receipt, so the card reads 1 contact and the row has no role, no email and a provenance line instead of a source. The count is honest and the record behind it is not what a contact usually means.

The page is accurate and it misleads

The fence was $4,800.00. Half moved by check before anyone opened the app, so the record shows $2,400.00 and every figure on the page is correct. Nothing here can know it is half the story, and no honest screen can say so, which is the price of the wedge rather than a defect in the layout.

Why Discover is last and looks different

It is the only thing on the page that serves the seller rather than Wes, so it goes at the bottom under a heading that says what it is. Three columns rather than three rows makes it read as a set of offers rather than as a checklist of things Wes has failed to do, which was the problem with the earlier version.

Jobs to be done, and what on the page serves them

  • J-04 Know what is owed and what has been collected. The money group, and at this tier it is the whole product. Outstanding $0.00, collected $2,400.00, and the payout is there so Wes can tie it to his bank statement.
  • J-09 Keep a relationship earning. This is the screen where a ten month old customer becomes a second sale. The record is thin and it still resolved from a name typed on a porch, which is the wedge's actual promise.
  • J-07 Walk into a conversation knowing what was said last time. Barely served, and honestly so. Four words and a date. There are no notes, because nobody has ever had anywhere to put one.
  • J-10 Start using the product without adopting all of it. The Discover row is the only place in the memo series where this job has a visible surface, and it is also the only advertising on a working screen.

Suggested copy, to be judged on its own

Button and menuDrawn as Take a payment. Alternatives: Collect a payment, slightly more formal and better if ACH arrives, or Charge a card, most specific and wrong the day the product takes anything else. Your New payment reads well in a menu but is not verb first, which the style guide asks of buttons and CTAs.
Discover headingUsed as you wrote it. If it needs to survive a narrow column, Do more with Pros or Grow with Pros carry the same idea in four words.
Overview footerNo email address, no home access info with an Add details link, naming both gaps rather than one, because the S15 record has a Home access info line and this one has nothing to put in it.
Declined rowRetried and approved in the status line, so a ten month old failure reads as resolved rather than as a live problem.

Open questions

  1. Should Wes have had to open this record at all? If taking a payment is the only thing he came for, a Take a payment action on the list row gets him there in one click and this page never opens.
  2. Does the nav shrink at this tier? The shell you sent ships one fixed nav and varies only the list columns by tier. Wes is looking at Pricebook, Financing, Analytics and Settings, none of which he has anything in.
  3. The previous work row has no status of its own. Paid is a money fact standing in for a work fact, and nothing in the record knows whether the fence was ever finished.
  4. Does a new payment attach to the existing project or mint a second one? The flow map says the gate is a second spine on the same anchors, so Take a payment quietly creates a project and nothing on this screen says so.
  5. Three rail events on one payment, on a customer record. Do they belong here, or on the payment with the customer showing one line?
  6. Overview holds a card called Overview. In your S15 record the group and the card share a name, which reads as a repetition once the group has only one or two cards in it.

Two things the files settled, and one they did not

Your own formatter already shows cents. The list recreation's money helper uses a minimum of two fraction digits, so a payments page renders $2,400.00. That matches the style guide and it means memos 10 through 13, which read $38,900 and $73,100, are the odd ones out rather than the rule.

Dates in the mock are US format. Jun 12, 2026 and Tue, Apr 13, 2027. The style guide says never day first, which is what the S15 memos do throughout.

What the files did not settle is the nav. The customer list varies its columns by tier, Payments, Selling and Operations, but the nav is one fixed list. So a payments-only contractor sees seven sections and has something in two of them, and this memo reproduces that rather than inventing a smaller nav.
