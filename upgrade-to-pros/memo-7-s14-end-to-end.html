S14 end to end: twenty beats, fourteen screens, and a customer who does not exist yet

This memo walks the whole of S14 rather than one moment of it, because the beats and the screens do not divide evenly: the first beat is three decisions in ninety seconds, and the hand-off beat is four screens in one paragraph. Fourteen sections, eleven drawn and three left as placeholders where the surface is a phone in somebody's hand. Corrected 14 Sep, having originally said ten and four; CD found it while building the frame table.

It is built from the scenario's What happens and Notes columns only. The Entities column on that page is generated rather than authored, so it is a reading of the team's model and not a specification, and nothing here is taken from it.

The thing it is testing is small and awkward: a project now comes into existence during a 7:50am phone call about a broken air conditioner, and nobody has decided whether anything on screen should say so.

Annotation inside a mockup is prefixed [i] and is never interface copy. Data is from the S14 worked example, not invented here. Editorial, not interactive.

Settled, for this memo

Working assumptions carried in, not things anybody has signed off

  • Demand starts at an inquiry. A customer contacts the contractor; that is an inquiry, and it becomes a lead when somebody qualifies it.
  • Creating the lead creates the project. In S14 that happens two minutes into the first call.
  • A lead carries interests. S14 has exactly one, which is why it is a good test: the distinction has to survive showing nothing.
  • Four status levels exist across inquiry, interest, lead and project. No screen here shows more than two of them.
  • The word Project need not appear. The container is called by its contents.
  • Money is switched off, not absent. Brightline has the capability and runs its cash through QuickBooks, which is different from a payments-only shop that never had it.
  • Job outer states are uniform and trade-specific steps sit inside them as sub-states.

To decide, before anything gets built

One thing, and it is not a layout question

  • Does anything tell anyone the project exists? It is created at 7:52am, it contains nothing, and it will contain nothing for two days. Three honest answers: name it on the lead from the first minute; show nothing until a proposal exists; or never show it at all and let the lead carry the whole of selling.
  • If the answer is "show nothing", that is a finding rather than a failure and it should be written down as one, because it means the container is a backend fact for the first two days of its life.
  • And if the honest answer turns out to be that nothing can be shown usefully until the hand-off, the model is wrong rather than the layout. A container created two minutes into a phone call, holding nothing, which no screen can justify mentioning, is a container created too early.

Everything else in this memo is arguable without settling this. Sections C, G and H are the three places it actually bites.

The Brenners are new, but the screen cannot know that until somebody looks. A near-match on the address is exactly what a CSR has to dismiss while a customer is talking, and if the interface does not show the near-match it will get dismissed by accident instead. One row, dismissed, with a reason.

It is the only badge on the screen and it earns its place under the density rule: a CSR needs to know they are about to create a customer before they read anything. Neutral tone, because nothing here needs acting on urgently.

No email, because Dana asked for a cell. Nothing about the property beyond the address. Unknown is written out rather than left blank, because a blank reads as unfinished and this is finished: it is everything that exists.

One button does four creations. Qualify makes a customer, a property, a lead and a project, and the screen names none of them. That is either the right amount of hiding or a dangerous amount, and I cannot tell without watching somebody use it.
