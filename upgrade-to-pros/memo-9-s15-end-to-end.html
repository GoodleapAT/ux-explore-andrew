Twelve screens, one customer, and an object that has never been drawn

This memo walks S15B from a quiet Monday when nothing is happening to the morning the money clears, and it is the first pass at the run end to end. It opens on the same page twice, a day apart, because everything the second one gains, it gains by itself. It is not really testing the screens. It is testing whether one set of components carries a three trade sale with the money on, having already carried a single trade sale with the money off, without breeding near identical siblings.

It runs against S15B, our variant, which now has a scenario page of its own so that S15 can be left alone. It is also the first time the Opportunity has been drawn at all. That object was settled today, it solves six modelling problems on paper, and until it is on a screen nobody knows whether it earns the room it takes.

[i] marks annotation. It belongs to this memo and never travels onto a canvas. Dashed regions are placeholders for things this round does not touch, and they are labelled. Each section carries the S15B beats it covers and who is on the screen.

Settled, for the purposes of this memo

Working assumptions carried in. None of it is ratified by anybody but Andrew.

  • The Opportunity is the durable record. One per customer, per property, per trade. Origin is an attribute, so a trade the customer asked for and a trade the system recommended are the same kind of record. It outlives every lead and every project.
  • Open, On a lead, Won, Dismissed. Won is the only terminal state. A trade that is offered and declined goes back to Open with a revive date and its history.
  • The Interest is retired. An opportunity is on a lead or it is not, and a proposal option prices it directly. Four demand grains, not five.
  • Qualification is automatic where a contact names a trade and a property. The lead and the project exist from the first contact, and nobody makes them.
  • A lead is one selling episode and it closes at the hand-off. In this run that is 24 August, six weeks before the project closes.
  • The money is drawn, in four states across five screens: no tender chosen, cash part paid, cash read-only at a job, and settled. S14 runs the same story with the money switched off, so this is the comparison that decides whether the two money cards should be one component.
  • The scenario is S15B, not S15. Ours differs from the team's in four named ways and now has a page of its own, so S15 can be left alone.
  • Two recommendations have been open for eleven months. Made two days after the heat pump job closed, off photographs Northgate's own crew took, and never mentioned to anybody until the intake call.

To decide, before anything is built

Two things, and a failure in the first means the model is wrong rather than the layout.

  • Does the opportunity survive being drawn? It appears on four of these seven of these twelve screens and it opens the whole strip. If that reads as clutter, or if a person cannot tell an opportunity from a lead at a glance, the object is wrong and not the page. It was settled on paper this afternoon and this is the first look at it.
  • Does a project delivering with no lead behind it have a template? Section H is a consequence of today's rulings and was not designed for: the lead closed at the hand-off and the project runs on for six weeks without it, so the only route back to how the sale happened is one row in a provenance card. Five of the twelve screens are in that state.
  • And does an eleven month old recommendation still belong on the page? Screens A and B open with two of them, and the memo takes no position on whether anything should age one out. Nothing in the model does. If the answer is that they should expire, the opportunity needs something it does not have.

Everything else that is open is listed at the foot, per the departure of 10 September.

A customer record with nothing going on is not an empty page. Two opportunities, a live service plan, a closed project and a thin trail of quiet activity. The page has content without having any demand on it.

A page can open on a record nobody made without saying so. The meta line reads "Home App, this morning" and "Unassigned", and neither of those is an apology. The lead and the project both exist here and the page mentions neither word.

The badge carries the eleven months, because that is the part a rep has to see before reading the row.

This is where the marking happens

The card is headed "Other opportunities at this property", not "worth raising", because nothing has been marked yet. The footer offers it. The previous version of this memo had the heading claim the marking was done while the footer offered to do it, which was the page arguing with itself.

There is no money card, and that is the rule working

No proposal, no contract, no invoice. A capability that will exist advertises and a gap the model cannot answer is left out, and this is neither: money will exist here and there is simply nothing in it yet, so the card is absent and the overview pairs with the property instead. Engage customer is the only real action on the page, and two of the three trades on it have never been mentioned to anybody. Nothing says which of the three routes is right when that is true, and nothing says what happens if you pick the wrong one.

  2. Is "Mark worth raising" a bulk action in the card footer, or a per row control? Drawn as the first. A per row control on a card with two rows is two controls doing one job.
  3. Where do dismissed opportunities go, now there is no filter? A third card would be three cards for one idea. Folding them into the second card contradicts its heading, since a dismissed trade is by definition not worth raising.
  4. At what count does the second card need its own filter back? Two is comfortable and twelve is not, and nothing says where the line is.

The claim, and it breaks a rule on purpose

Three collapsible sections, roofing open. The composition standard says detail that does not fit goes sideways into a sheet, not down into an accordion, and this goes down into an accordion. Declared here rather than done quietly, which is what a Default marker asks for.

Why the rule loses here

A segmented control hides two trades behind a click while somebody is talking. The customer answers about all three in one conversation and does not take them in order, so a person typing has to be able to reach any of them without losing what is on screen. A sheet would cover the transcript, which is worse.

The counts moved onto the headers

"Siding, 4 of 6 answered" on a collapsed section. The card still answers its question before anything is opened, which is the thing the segmented control was carrying and the thing an accordion usually loses.

What it cost, and this is the reason to watch it

The nested block has gone. Siding and windows used to appear inside the roofing card as a subordinate list, showing what the customer said about each. That content is now behind two closed headers, so the page no longer says on its face that the customer was keen on one and lukewarm on the other.

Why the transcript is on the left

Andrew asked for it in place of the trade picker. It is on the left because the person is reading it and typing on the right, and reading left while typing right is the only arrangement that does not cross the hands.

Nothing converts here

Both stay Open. The detail is captured against them and the customer has engaged, but they do not go on the lead until Start selling, which is section F. That is C's reading and it is what gives the six days something to be.

This screen is crowded and it is the second warning

Three regions compete: the transcript, the three trade sections, and the page furniture. Andrew flagged the same crowding on the S14 capture screen. It is worse here because there are three trades rather than one.

Every section gets a notes field

The template asks what it can ask, and the useful half of a call is always the part no field anticipated. One free box per trade, so the note lands against the opportunity it is about rather than in a single pile at the end.

The not-asked treatment has gone with the field that carried it

A dashed "Not asked" field was the only demonstration of it, and removing that field removes the demonstration. The treatment is still in the component record and still right, and this memo no longer shows it.

This is the front desk, not the sales consultant

Renata takes the call and hands the lead to Priya afterwards. That is a change to the worked example rather than to this screen, and it puts a hand-off into the gap before Start selling that previously had nothing in it.

Open

  1. Is the transcript worth the width it takes? It is half the screen and it is a capability nobody has confirmed exists. The cheap alternative is a collapsed strip that expands.
  2. Does the temperature belong on the collapsed header? "Siding, keen" would say what matters commercially, where "4 of 6 answered" says how full the form is. The second is drawn because it does not change while somebody types.
  3. Where does the call itself live afterwards? Communications has surfaced six times in two days and is still a register row.

The claim

A card per job, each answering three questions: where it is, what is next, what it is waiting on. Two jobs abreast is the pairing rule doing the work the nesting was supposed to do, and a reader compares them by looking rather than by expanding anything.
